# A440 Acoustic Standard & Concert Pitch Specification

A detailed reference on the international acoustic standard ISO 16, historical pitch standards, and practical methods for reference calibration.

---

## 1. The Specification

In modern acoustics, **Concert Pitch A440** defines that the fundamental frequency of the musical note **A₄** (the fourth A on an 88-key piano, situated nine semitones above Middle C at MIDI note 69) shall vibrate at:

```
f(A4) = 440.00 Hz  (cycles per second)
```

### International Codification
- **Standard**: ISO 16:1975 (*Acoustics — Standard tuning frequency*)
- **Reference Temperature**: 20.0°C (68.0°F)
- **Tolerance**: Professional orchestral standards generally require pitch tolerance within ±0.5 Hz (≈ 2 cents).

---

## 2. Historical Context: The Evolution to 440 Hz

Standard pitch is a twentieth-century harmonization. Prior to international treaties, musical pitch varied widely across geographies and centuries:

| Era / Location | Standard Name | Frequency of A4 | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Baroque (17th–18th c.)** | Kammerton (Chamber pitch) | ~415 Hz | Roughly a semitone lower than modern A440 |
| **Baroque Organ** | Chorton (Choir pitch) | ~466 Hz | High organ pitch designed for smaller pipe metal volume |
| **Classical Vienna (1780)** | Mozart's tuning fork | 421.6 Hz | Mellow, less string tension |
| **France (1859)** | Diapason Normal | 435.0 Hz | Codified into French civil law |
| **19th c. London & Vienna** | "High Pitch" / Concert pitch | 452.5 Hz | Aggressive brightness in large romantic halls |
| **London Conference (1939)** | International Standard | 440.0 Hz | Compromise between French 435 Hz and British/US 440 Hz |
| **Modern European Orchestras** | Vienna, Berlin Philharmonic | 442.0 – 443.0 Hz | Modern aesthetic preference for extra string brilliance |

## 3. Acoustic Physics: Calculating Interference Beats

When two instruments play near A440, the superposition of their sound waves creates an amplitude modulation phenomenon known as **acoustic beats**.

If a reference tone emits frequency f1 and an instrument emits frequency f2, the beat frequency heard by the ear is:

```
f_beat = |f1 - f2|
```

- **Example 1**: An uncalibrated violin string vibrates at 443 Hz against a 440 Hz reference. The ear perceives a 3 Hz pulsing sensation (3 beats per second).
- **Example 2**: As the peg is loosened and the string approaches 440.2 Hz, the beat frequency slows to 0.2 Hz (one gentle swell every five seconds).
- **At Zero Beats**: When f_beat = 0, phase coherence is achieved and the two notes lock into acoustic unison.
